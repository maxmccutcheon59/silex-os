# Company

Silex is the software layer every robot, cell, and physical agent has to call before it is allowed to act.

Windows sold an operating system to hardware companies. The PC makers kept the metal. Microsoft kept the permission to run programs. That is the shape.

Today the hardware is Fanuc, ABB, AMRs, humanoids, and whatever comes next. The missing OS is not another motion controller. It is authorization, completion, and proof: who may do what, on which resource, under which policy, with a record that closes the work or halts it.

AI belongs *behind* that OS. Models propose. Silex decides whether the world may move.

## What we are

- Software sold to plants and, later, to OEM / integrator hardware companies
- Vendor-neutral. Compatible is a feature. Exclusive is a trap.
- High-margin, high-switching-cost infrastructure

## What we are not

- A robot manufacturer
- A foundation-model lab
- A copilot chat company
- A single-OEM add-on

## Elon-shaped innovation (without owning the metal)

First principles on *control*, not on building another body.

- Iterate the permission loop weekly. Hardware iterates yearly.
- Every consequential action is a writ or it did not happen.
- Failed work rolls back. Incomplete work does not close.
- Replay real cell logs before buying or blessing new hardware.
- When humanoids show up, they boot into the same plane as a 2014 arm.

The innovation is making unauthorized physical action illegitimate — the way unsigned drivers became illegitimate on a serious OS.

## Who pays

1. Plants: site license so quality and safety can close a work order.
2. Integrators: certified adapters.
3. OEMs: "Silex-compatible" the way PCs were "Windows-compatible."

If an OEM will not call your writ API, you are still a tool. When they must, you are the OS.

## Equity

Software only. Dual-class at incorporation. Small option pool. Revenue from plants before a priced round. No robot capex.
